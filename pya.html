Amazon · Loyalty

Prime for Young Adults

Reinventing a struggling loyalty program for Gen Z, reversing five years of membership decline by replacing free shipping messaging with cash back, built around joy, social currency, and winning.

Role · Sole Designer, US Prime CX
Scope · 8+ surfaces, sign-up through redemption
Status · Launched, driving post-launch optimization

The situation

Prime Student had shrunk from 4.6MM members in 2020 to 3.4MM by December 2024, churning faster than Amazon could acquire new members, despite adding 3.9MM signups a year.

Nearly half of Gen Z was actively trying to shop less on Amazon, and their discretionary income had declined 22% year-over-year. A discounted price of $7.49/month wasn't enough to convince them to pay for their own membership. The program didn't need a refresh. It needed a reinvention.

The case for reinvention

Before any design work began, the case had to be built. A PR/FAQ grounded in quantitative research and competitive analysis found that 35% of young adults cited cash back and discounts as their top priority, nearly twice the next category. Free shipping, Prime's traditional core value prop, was mentioned by only 15%: for this generation, shipping is table stakes, not a differentiator.

Cash back was chosen because it can't be replicated by sharing a parent's account, it creates a personal accumulation loop that motivates return visits, and it directly counters the price-driven appeal of Temu and SHEIN. The PR/FAQ was reviewed at CEO-level and the reinvention was greenlit, establishing three design tenets as the north star for the work.

Tenet

Joy

Surprise drops and gamification, moments that feel delightful rather than transactional.

Tenet

Social currency

Sharable, exclusive, something worth telling friends about.

Tenet

Winning

Hustling the system, getting a deal, a feeling of coming out ahead.

Key design decisions

Vision concepts that leaned hardest into joy and social currency, a gifting flow, a countdown for upcoming drops, a sharable cash back moment, were pushed to post-launch: deprioritized for tech constraints and timeline, not for lacking merit.

Discovery

In-context over a dedicated destination

Research showed Gen Z has less than 8 seconds of active attention, so cash back awareness was distributed across the existing shopping journey rather than a hub they'd never visit. A Prime-branded badge system was envisioned but shipped as generic savings messaging due to tech constraints, a gap Layne hypothesizes is driving the redemption shortfall.

Scalability

A hub built to configure, not rebuild

Built scalable components that could be configured on the page, cutting marketing lead time from 4 weeks to 2 and engineering workload from 5 days to 1, absorbing requirements from Legal, Finance, Marketing, and Prime leadership without letting scope expand.

Workaround

A shoppable hub in place of a static sign-up page

The existing sign-up page had tech limits and no in-app path due to Apple App Store restrictions. Routing non-Prime members to the configurable, shoppable hub let them see the cash back value in action instead of just reading about it.

Continuity

No dead ends

Every point where a customer diverged from the acquisition flow, detail page, cart, checkout, always carried a join-Prime opportunity with the 5% cash back message present. Every path back to shopping still had a path into membership.

Both accelerated well past pre-launch rates of +6.1% and +3.9% respectively. The cash back system reached near-universal adoption: 5MM members earning, 99% of the member balance, $117MM earned, $43MM redeemed.

Post-launch experiments compounded the gains

5 of 8 post-launch experiments won, adding +8.7K annualized paid members on top of the larger surface wins. The checkout redemption improvement alone drove +$161M/yr in incremental customer sales, +102K annualized Prime members, and saved 56K members from churn annually. A more prominent Search badge added +$9M/yr, the profile tab upsell added +21K signups, and the Membership Central cash back value prop added +12K annualized paid members, outperforming Same-Day delivery as a value message.

What's next

The MVP optimized for the earning side of cash back. The spending side, redemption visibility, balance awareness, value celebration, is where the largest gaps remain.

Fix redemption visibility

99% of members earn cash back, but only 46.4% have redeemed. Until recently, the only redemption surface was the Payment Selection Page, a screen most shoppers click through on autopilot. The checkout experiment proved visibility drives behavior, and a personalized balance card on the homepage Prime benefits widget added +$4M/yr on its own. The next step is making the balance a persistent element across the hub, homepage, profile tab, checkout, and potentially Search, plus a "use your cash back" nudge at the moment of highest intent and a celebratory SMS moment the first time a member crosses the $5 redemption threshold.

+$161M/yr from checkout visibility
+56K members saved from churn
+$4M/yr from homepage balance card

Enhance acquisition messaging

The free-trial message ("$0 for 6 months") beat 5% cash back messaging by 30.8K annualized signups on the homepage, and a "Cash Back" nav pill actually hurt signups. Members describe cash back as the reason they stay, not the reason they signed up. The opportunity is to lead acquisition with the free trial as the low-risk entry point, reserve cash back messaging for post-signup engagement, and reframe the sign-up page around identity, "what kind of person is this for", rather than the percentages and terms it leads with today.

AI-assisted ideation

Layne also used AI tools to help generate and pressure-test additional growth directions against the original tenets, streak and milestone mechanics, limited-time category unlocks, a lighter-weight verification flow, surfacing the cash back balance at the moment of cancellation, a personalized year-in-review moment, tiered rewards for loyalty duration, and a referral program. These are earlier-stage explorations, not yet scoped or validated.
