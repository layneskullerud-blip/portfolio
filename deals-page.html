The problem space

Years of incremental additions left the page without a cohesive vision, and Prime Day 2026 exposed exactly where it was cracking.

01

Customers can't search or browse their way to relevant deals

A one-size-fits-all ranking model shows every customer the same deals, based on forecasted sales rather than who they are.

77% expected personalization55% felt they got it "I feel like you have to go through a lot of stuff you're not interested in to get to just a couple items you may be interested in."
02

The experience is overwhelming

Two competing navigation systems, tabs and a secondary visual navigation layer, add cognitive load instead of reducing it.

~90% only use the first 3 of 20+ navigation tabs
03

Browsers get no inspiration

A uniform ASIN grid is built for transactional efficiency, not for the browsing and inspiration-seeking most visitors actually want.

44% browse with no specific intent27% actively seek inspiration
04

The page ignores journey context

No adaptation for entry point, customer type, or shopping mission, a huge share of visitors land on a degraded, disconnected experience.

35–40% arrive from Homepage recommenders

What Prime Day 2026 taught us

Layne had already designed enhancements for the Deals Page CX that launched for Prime Day 2026, and the live results from that event are directly shaping the vision.

Personalized ranking works

Every personalized ranking treatment increased both deal sales and annualized sales, with 100% probability of a positive result. Clear signal to scale worldwide.

Deal sales
$27.7M → $31M
Annualized sales
$46.8M → $62.8M

Navigation exposes a personalization gap

New mobile navigation more than doubled category filter usage, effectively a search-refinement action, but because filtered results weren't personalized, US deal sales dropped. Customers want to narrow their experience without losing relevance.

Filter clicks
1.6M → 3.5M
US deal sales
−$2.99M

Conversational AI needs to stay in-context

The "Ask Alexa" ingress, which sent customers off the page, had neutral impact. The hypothesis: AI needs to live inside the shopping context, not act as an off-ramp.

Deal sales
$657K (neutral)

Broad discovery without personalization hurts

Replacing personalized shovelers with non-personalized collections, and pushing the grid below the fold, cost real value. Anything that replaces a personalized surface must itself be personalized.

Annualized sales / visit
−13% YoY

The design philosophy

Resolving the tension between personalization and discovery through a layered approach.

Foundation

Personalization as the foundation, not the ceiling

The grid, the navigation, and even the inspiration content all become personally relevant, proven by the Prime Day 2026 ranking results.

Adaptation

Context-aware adaptation

The page adapts by entry point, customer type, and mindset, building on Layne's earlier journey-aware design principles. It also adapts to what's being shopped for: a high-consideration purchase like a mattress or TV calls for a different pace and depth than a low-cost, frequently repurchased item like groceries or everyday essentials.

AI

AI as an accessible layer, not a replacement

Conversational prompts help customers refine intent, but the core experience never requires AI engagement, the same guided-over-chatbot instinct from SoleMate.

Simplicity

Unified simplicity

One navigation, not two, so search and browse behavior work the same way whether it's an everyday visit or a peak event like Prime Day or Black Friday Cyber Monday. Fewer navigation tabs, not more. Trust cues on faceouts so customers can act without leaving the page.

The hard part

Designing for a $244B/year surface, with live experiment data landing mid-process, for three VPs who each care about something different. This is the fourth SVP-level presentation in a year of increasing scope across the promotions and savings space.

"Nine themes, three VPs, one coherent story." Merchandising, pricing trust, and overall customer experience all have to read as a single unified vision, not a feature list, inside a six-week design window.

Owning design vision for one of Amazon's highest-traffic surfaces, translating live experiment data into forward-looking CX direction, and aligning three VPs on what Amazon's savings experience should be. The vision now moves into an MVP CX, targeted to launch for the next major sale event in October to validate the direction with real customers.
