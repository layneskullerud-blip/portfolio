Creator Storefronts for Everyone

Taking the Creator Storefront experience, built for professional influencers, and bringing it to every Amazon customer, so sharing what you love with friends and family is as easy as sharing a product page.

Role · Lead Designer, with one supporting designer
Scope · CX companion + mocks for L10 review
Status · Launched to 5M US customers

The opportunity

Sharing products is already prevalent on Amazon, customers just couldn't create anything richer than a detail-page link. In 2024, the social share button was used 4x more (+28.5% YoY), driving $30B in iOPS (+10% YoY). The vision: make it just as easy to create and share real product content, photos, videos, reviews, idea lists, in a way that's rewarding when others find value in what you share.

Who this is for

Three customer use cases anchored the design: people who already create and share product moments organically, just without a good tool for it.

Josephine

The weekly player

Plays tennis at least once a week and shares outfit and gear photos with friends. Uploads photos to auto-tag every product, then texts or shares the shoppable link.

Eloise

The curator

Spends hours building beauty collages for her college sorority sisters. Purchased products are automatically turned into fun, shareable collages.

Peter

The book clubber

A Kindle subscriber running a local book club. Texts or emails one link to a page holding all his reviews and auto-generated idea lists.

What was designed

Lead designer for the full CX, presented to L10 leadership. The work spanned three connected pieces.

Customer profile
Migrated customer profiles into the Creator Storefront infrastructure: four content types (photos, videos, idea lists, reviews), like and share, pinned content, and an immersive feed aligned to Creator Storefronts
Content creation
An AI-generated content widget customers swipe through to publish personalized, seasonal draft content, plus product auto-tagging, AI-generated titles and captions, and idea lists built from a few words
Discovery
Customer content surfaces to drive product discovery for others through the Storefront experience, measured with OPS-driven metrics as it publishes to broader surfaces
Review
CX companion and mocks presented to L10 leadership as part of the program's executive review

The philosophy

The key strategic call: extend paid creator features to customers rather than build bespoke customer features from scratch, holding off on dedicated investment until data justified it.

Reuse

Extend, don't rebuild

Creator tools were more mature and could be adapted cheaply, balancing the size of the opportunity (5M customers, 10x content growth) against resource reality.

Discovery

Incremental, not a new feed

Customers who meet the quality bar benefit from incremental discovery efforts. No separate Inspire-like feed built for customer content.

Positioning

Complement, don't replace

Creator Storefronts for Everyone complements Social Share rather than replacing it, a richer option sitting alongside the simple share button, not competing with it.

Restraint

No bespoke features, yet

No custom customer-only features until data shows exactly where customer behavior diverges from creator behavior.

The hard part

Recovering from a CEO-level escalation

A CEO-level escalation in March 2025 flagged confusing UX: unintuitive entry points, navigation issues, and naming that didn't match how customers actually thought about the feature. Worked through rapid fixes, defaulting to the tab with content, hiding empty tabs, renaming the experience, updating entry points, to resolve the feedback.

Defining "minimum loveable," not just "minimum viable"

The launch reached 5M customers and 160K content pieces, but real gaps remained between what shipped and a genuinely delightful experience. Led a deep dive to prioritize the features that deliver step-change value, making content creation easy, fun, and motivating, rather than just functional.

Bridging two different mental models

Creator Storefronts were built for people creating content professionally. Adapting that infrastructure for casual customers, who share products organically rather than as a business, meant rethinking onboarding, empty states, motivational cues, and what "success" looks like for someone who isn't optimizing for commissions.

Connection to broader work

Creator Storefronts for Everyone sits at the intersection of three programs, three years of social commerce design converging into one customer-facing product.

Amazon Discover

Sole designer for the shoppable content feed that customer-generated content now flows into. Shoppable image cards, content-to-product tagging, and heart/save mechanics carry over directly.

Shoppable Collages

The collage concept originated during Discover, product collages with stickers, text, and backgrounds, is directly reflected in the AI-generated collage feature here.

Generative AI Fashion Shopping

The vision of customers as creators, designing, sharing, and shopping each other's content, realized here through a more pragmatic vehicle.
